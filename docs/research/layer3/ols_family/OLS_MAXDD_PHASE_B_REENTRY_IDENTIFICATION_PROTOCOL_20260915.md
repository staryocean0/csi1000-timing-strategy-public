## 1. Why Phase B exists

Phase A found that the largest drawdowns are concentrated in a small number of episodes and that repeated participation/re-entry is positively associated with drawdown depth across all five frozen exit families. It also found that fit-R² and path-efficiency collapse are especially strong in persistent-tail episodes, with frozen-midline showing the strongest confidence-collapse signatures.

Phase B does **not** change trading. Its only question is whether the dangerous state can be recognized causally before a new executable segment is allowed to add further drawdown.

## 2. Primary question

> At the decision close immediately before each new executable non-flat segment, do observable churn, prior-confidence damage, apparent confidence recovery, and existing underwater state consistently predict additional drawdown generated by the upcoming segment?

The purpose is to establish or reject early-identification mechanisms for a later Phase C intervention study. Phase B does not choose a cooldown, entry block, exit rule, size, or threshold.

A segment is a maximal consecutive run of the same non-zero executable position. If executable position begins on bar `t`, the causal entry snapshot is the decision close `t-1`; candidate state features must use only information available by that close.

## 4. Primary future-risk outcome

For each segment, Phase B records `future_drawdown_extension`:

- measure strategy drawdown at the segment execution point;
- follow only the naturally occurring frozen-baseline segment;
- measure the worst strategy drawdown reached during that segment;
- `future_drawdown_extension = max(0, |worst future drawdown| - |drawdown at execution|)`.

This is the primary adverse outcome. No fixed future-bar horizon is selected.

Secondary outcomes are segment maximum adverse excursion, segment gross return, and whether the segment sets a new deeper equity trough.

## 5. Frozen causal candidate features

All risk directions below are preregistered as **higher value = higher expected future risk**.

1. `reentry_ordinal_since_hwm` — count of prior segment starts since the last causally observable lagged equity high-water mark.
2. `lagged_drawdown_abs_at_decision` — absolute strategy drawdown known at the decision close, using only returns already realized before that decision.
3. `prior_r2_max_collapse` — prior completed segment's maximum peak-to-current fit-R² collapse.
4. `prior_pe_max_collapse` — prior completed segment's maximum peak-to-current path-efficiency collapse.
5. `reentry_speed` — `1 / (1 + flat_gap_bars)` between the prior completed segment and the new segment.
6. `false_confidence_r2` — `prior_r2_max_collapse * entry_signal_r2`; high values mean large recent R² damage followed by an apparently strong new R² fit.
7. `false_confidence_pe` — `prior_pe_max_collapse * entry_signal_pe`.
8. `churn_damage_r2` — `reentry_ordinal_since_hwm * prior_r2_max_collapse`.

`entry_signal_r2`, `entry_signal_pe`, and entry slope are read from the entering side/window on decision bar `t-1`, not from future executable-bar information.

## 6. Analysis plan

For every candidate and exit family, Phase B will report:

- Spearman association with `future_drawdown_extension` across eligible segment starts;
- the same association restricted to underwater re-entry segments;
- direction-adjusted rank AUC for identifying the worst 20% of `future_drawdown_extension` within that family;
- per-year association for family-year cells with sufficient observations;
- sign-stability across years;
- descriptive comparisons of first participation after a high-water mark versus later underwater re-entry.

The 20% outcome tail is an evaluation label only. It is not a proposed trading threshold.

## 7. Frozen Phase-C promotion gate

For gate conditions 1–4, the **primary eligible universe is underwater re-entry rows**: `lagged_drawdown_abs_at_decision > 0` and `reentry_ordinal_since_hwm >= 1`, with the candidate itself finite. This prevents the gate from being passed merely by contrasting ordinary first participation with already-underwater repeated participation.

A candidate mechanism becomes `PHASE_C_ELIGIBLE` only if all conditions hold:

1. expected-risk-direction Spearman `rho >= 0.25` in at least 3 of 5 exit families on the primary eligible universe;
2. the association has the expected positive sign in at least 4 of 5 exit families;
3. median direction-adjusted family AUC for the worst-20%-extension label is at least `0.60` across families with sufficient observations;
4. among sufficient family-year cells, at least `70%` have the expected positive association;
5. the feature is verified to be causally available at decision close `t-1`.

`phase_c_authority=true` only if at least one frozen candidate passes. Passing authorizes only a separately preregistered Phase C intervention comparison. It does not authorize production or direct trading use.

## 8. Sufficiency rules

- Family-level correlation/AUC requires at least 20 eligible rows and at least two distinct candidate values.
- Family-year sign stability requires at least 10 eligible rows and at least two distinct candidate values.
- Missing prior-segment features remain missing for rows where no completed prior segment exists; they are not imputed.

## 9. Anti-overfitting boundaries

Phase B must not:

- search R², PE, cooldown, holding, or sizing thresholds;
- change entry/exit formulas;
- suppress or add trades;
- optimize candidate weights;
- select a feature by total PnL;
- claim fresh OOS;
- use Phase-B outcomes to alter this promotion gate after the run.

Negative findings are authoritative and must be retained.
